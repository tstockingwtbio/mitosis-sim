## 1. Style sheet

Every image prompt starts with the style block below, and every prompt that shows Fig adds the character lock. Upload the approved "Paper-Cutout Newt Rescue Station" render as a reference image with every ChatGPT request.

### Style block (paste first, every time)

> Layered cut-paper diorama built from smooth matte cardstock and construction paper, photographed straight on. 5 to 7 distinct layers raised on foam spacers; each layer casts a clear, sharp drop shadow onto the layer behind it. Crisp scissor-cut edges with a thin pale paper core showing at the edge, faint paper grain, slight curl at thin tips, occasional tiny creases. Every paper piece has a thick, hand-inked, near-black brown (#2B1D14) outline along its cut edge, in a bold comic cel-shaded style. Palette: teal, warm orange, cream, deep blue, moss green; high contrast. Flat, even studio light from the upper left. No felt, no fabric, no fuzz. No text, no lettering, no numbers, no logos.

### Character lock: Fig (add whenever she appears)

> The same newt as the reference image: orange body with dark slate-blue mottled patches on the back, cream belly, large half-lidded eyes with a mildly unimpressed expression, a tail banded in orange and slate blue, exactly 4 toes on each front foot and 5 toes on each back foot.

### Rules for every asset

- **One image per request.** Asking for several in one prompt causes style drift.
- **Never ask the image for words, numbers, or labels.** All text is added in code so it can switch languages.
- **Character cutouts** (people, newts, the heron) go on a transparent background, so the code can layer them.
- **Text-safe backgrounds** (behind on-screen text or the model) keep the right 40% of the frame plain, with half the usual number of objects.
- **Story panels** may be busy; they appear only between tasks, never during one.
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 for scenes and backgrounds, 1:1 for cutouts and textures.
- **Outline weight:** thick on characters and scenery, thinner on small props. Check every render against the reference image before accepting it.
- **Science check before accepting:** toe counts, chromosome shapes (if any appear), and nothing that contradicts the model built in code. For IMG-B10 to B13: the nucleus shows loose threads (not rods or X shapes); the chromosome is a single rod (not an X); the DNA has exactly two strands with a right-handed twist and rungs made of two halves.

## 2. Story bible

One newt carries both units. In Part A (Unit 3) Fig regrows her tail, and students learn that every cell in her body carries identical DNA. In Part B (Unit 4) a DNA test says Fig is not the mother of her own hatchlings, and the only explanation breaks that rule: Fig is a chimera.

### Setting

Mossbank Amphibian Rescue Station: a small, cluttered research station beside a pond, home to rescued ember newts (a fictional species). The station has a tank room, a lab bench with a DNA machine, a hatchery, an old UV-lamp room, and an evidence board where the mystery gets pinned up.

### Tone guide

- **Funny about the biology, never beside it.** Every joke points at the idea on screen.
- **Humor is situational and visual,** not puns or idioms, so it survives translation.
- **Footnotes are optional.** They open with a tap, never during a task.
- **Sentences stay short.** Building lines run 2 or 3 sentences at most; Beginning lines use one simple sentence.
- **Nobody is a fool.** The humans are wrong for good reasons, which is the point of the mystery.

### Part B beats (Unit 4, SC.HS.9.4.b)

| Beat | Sim module | What happens | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| B7 | Epilogue (optional, teacher-led) | Narrator: "Fig is, technically, her own aunt." A short note that chimerism also occurs in people. | Real-world transfer. |

### Science notes for the teacher

- **The model organism has 2n = 4.** Real newts have far more chromosomes; the A4 limits card says so.
- **Natural chimerism** is documented in mammals, including people. In amphibians it is mostly made in labs by grafting embryos, so Fig is fiction built on real biology.
- **The human case (B7)** involved fraud accusations and children placed in foster care. Keep the epilogue to the biology; leave out the legal story.
- **A2b uses a 24-hour cycle** as a round number for the calculation. Real cycle lengths vary by species and tissue.
- **"Share of cells = share of time"** only holds if cells divide independently at random times. The A4 limits card names this assumption.
- **Phase names stay on reference cards** in A2b. Students are assessed on the reasoning (why most cells are in interphase, how counts become times), not on naming phases from memory, which the 6.1.d boundary excludes.
- **A2b misconceptions to target:** interphase is "resting" (DNA is copied then); phases are separate steps (the cycle is continuous); a phase with more cells is faster (it is slower).
- **A2b Go further prompt:** predict the counts if a chemical stopped cells at metaphase (cells pile up there), which shows that growth is controlled by regulating the cycle.

## 3. Voice-over script

The narrator voices every story line; characters speak only in on-screen speech bubbles, which the narrator reads when read-aloud is on. Building lines are recorded in English. Beginning lines are recorded in all 9 languages from translated scripts. That makes 39 lines × (1 + 9) = 390 clips for the story alone.

### Narrator voice

- **English (Building):** warm, dry, curious narrator in their late 40s with a clear General American accent; calm natural pace; amused rather than jokey. Eleven v3.
- **Direction tags:** type them into the text exactly as written. One emotion tag at the start of the words it colors; [pause] right before a punchline or a key term; at most 3 or 4 tags per line, or v3 starts to sound erratic. If a take reads a tag aloud, regenerate it.
- **Other languages (Beginning):** a native-speaker voice from the Voice Library for each language, same warm and clear delivery, slower pace, [pause] tags only.
- **Dari and Farsi** get separate scripts and, if possible, separate voices. Have a Dari speaker check that the Dari audio doesn't sound Iranian.

## 6. Built in code, not generated

Everything students count, move, compare or read is built in Three.js, so it stays scientifically exact, changes state on demand, and relabels in any language. These pieces use the paper textures (IMG-T01 to T07) and the same ink outline, so they match the generated art.

### Animation rules

- **Paper motion:** pieces slide, flip and snap like paper on a desk; no squash-and-stretch on the model.
- **Reduced motion:** parallax, paper flutter and camera fly-ins are replaced by cuts and fades.
- **No timers:** every animation can be paused, replayed and stepped through one stage at a time.
