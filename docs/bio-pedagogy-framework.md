# Biology Pedagogy Framework (single source of truth)

Apply to every Biology (grade 9, all abilities, includes English learners) and AP Biology (dual-enrolled, first-year college level) material. This skill governs PEDAGOGY. Format/layout skills (worksheet templates, item writers, test assemblers) govern appearance; load this first, then those.

## 0. Before building, state in one line
Course | module ID (e.g., EV-04) | standard + its action verb + scope limits | target type (procedure/vocab OR conceptual) | sequencing choice (see 2).

## 1. Central commitment
Teach prerequisite knowledge explicitly and efficiently, then progressively transfer responsibility to students for interpreting evidence, designing investigations, explaining mechanisms, and applying biology to unfamiliar cases. Guidance is always present; never unassisted discovery.

## 2. Sequencing rule (choose per learning target, never globally)
- New vocabulary, procedure, or representation -> explicit first: brief explanation, think-aloud, one worked example, guided practice, independent practice. Fade steps over time.
- Conceptual target AND students have adequate prior knowledge (e.g., natural selection mechanism, enzyme function) -> problem/phenomenon first with a structured attempt, then consolidating explicit instruction that contrasts student attempts with the canonical model.
- Log the choice in teacher notes so pilot results can be compared across sequences.

## 3. 45-minute module (planning default, NOT research-optimal)
Retrieve 5 | Explain/model 10 | Guided practice + feedback 10 | Apply/investigate 15 | Independent check 5. Labs may use most of the period. Two modules pair for 90-min days. Constants: learning goal, prior knowledge, purposeful practice, individual evidence.

## 4. Five material categories (shared visual language)
OBSERVE (phenomenon, predictions) | DECODE (guided notes, models) | INVESTIGATE (lab, sim, data collection) | CONNECT (transfer, data analysis, argument, ACT passage) | CHECKPOINT (exit ticket, quiz). Recurring functions, not mandatory sections: Today's Goal; Retrieve; Key Idea; Learn & Practice (or activity-specific middle); Show What You Know.

## 5. Required in every material (checklist)
1. Retrieve block: >=3 free-recall or short-answer items drawn from (a) last module, (b) last week, (c) a prior unit. Include answer feedback for the teacher.
2. Worked example precedes independent practice for any new procedure; later materials fade steps.
3. Check for understanding: all-student response plus a stated teacher action for common wrong answers. Exit item is independent.
4. Misconception: name the predicted wrong idea, contrast it with evidence, revisit later.
5. Integrated representations: explanatory text next to its diagram/graph (no split attention); highlight key relationships.
6. Coherence: no decorative images inside the working area. Header/footer styling is fine. Every visual must carry information.
7. Evidence of mastery matches the standard's verb and scope boundaries. Completion and engagement are not mastery.
8. Tag each item/task with its module ID and standard so exit-ticket error patterns can be analyzed later.

## 6. Differentiation and language (built in for everyone)
- Same core task for all; supports are embedded, not separate easier/harder versions: tiered sentence frames, Tier 2/3 vocabulary list with visuals, optional hint cards, and an optional extension prompt in the same format.
- Regular Bio: pitch for ALL ability levels at 9th grade. AP Bio: pitch for first-year college students, fewer worked steps, more independence in experimental design and evaluation.
- Scenarios use varied names/contexts that need no specific cultural background.

## 7. Accessibility
Default font Atkinson Hyperlegible. Never signal meaning by color alone: pair color with icon, label, or pattern. Keep adequate contrast. Equal padding between nested rounded buttons/labels and their card edge.

## 8. Assessments
- Unfamiliar context, one standard per item, scope boundaries honored (e.g., no Hardy-Weinberg unless in scope).
- Do not treat a trait-frequency increase as proof of adaptation, or a time trend as proof of causation. Distinguish phenotype proportions from allele frequencies.
- Distractors roughly equal length; correct answer not systematically longest; answer letters irregularly distributed, rarely repeating back-to-back.
- Include cumulative/spaced items from earlier units.
- Pair selected response with short constructed response (explain mechanism, interpret dataset, evaluate claim).

## 9. Measurement
Plan a pre/post diagnostic and a delayed retrieval check about 4-6 weeks after each unit. Record what took longer, what students misunderstood, which standards were independently shown. Revise from student work.

## 10. ACT routine
About 12 minutes every other week inside an existing module: 2 strategy, 6 independent passage, 3 review, 1 error reflection. Original, unit-aligned passages. Justify as data-interpretation practice, not as ACT score prep.

## 11. Citations
When stating real scientific findings from peer-reviewed journals, cite in CSE citation-sequence with a References section. Not needed for pedagogy choices inside the document.

## 12. Before delivering
Run checklist 5-8 silently. Report ONE line: "Framework check: passed" or "flagged: <item>". Do not narrate the framework to the teacher.

## Evidence anchors (calibrated)
Retrieval and spacing (IES practice guide; Yang et al. 2021 meta-analysis). Explicit instruction and worked examples (IES; EEF Improving Secondary Science). Guided beats unassisted discovery (Alfieri et al. 2011, J Educ Psychol 103(1):1-18). Problem-first-then-instruction can beat instruction-first for conceptual targets with adequate prior knowledge (Sinha & Kapur 2021, Rev Educ Res 91(5):761-798). Decorative content can reduce retention and transfer (Rey 2012 meta-analysis). Time allocations, five categories, and module names are implementation conventions, not validated interventions.
